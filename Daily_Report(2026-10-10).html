🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 9 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures fell to a two-month low on Friday on spillover pressure from sharply lower corn futures and after the US Department of Agriculture increased its US wheat supply outlook in a monthly crop report.

    • The US Department of Agriculture on Friday raised its US wheat ending stocks forecast for the 2026/27 season to 740 million bushels, up from its 717-million-bushel outlook last month and above the average analyst estimate of 721 million.

    • The USDA trimmed its 2026/27 US wheat acreage forecast but raised its average yield outlook, resulting in a 3-million-bushel production increase. US wheat exports for 2026/27 were cut by 25 million bushels.

    • Wheat futures remain underpinned by global supply worries as shipments from the Black Sea region remain restricted by the Russia-Ukraine war.

    • In its monthly report on Friday, the USDA lowered its outlook for Russian wheat exports by 3 million metric tons for the 2026/27 season. That was partly offset by small increases to Argentine and Canadian exports.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 closed 12-1/4 cents lower at $6.71 per bushel.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 last traded 17 cents lower at $7.19-1/4 a bushel.

    • Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 ended 11-1/4 cents lower at $6.95-1/4 a bushel.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 9 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures plunged by as much as the daily trading limit on Friday and settled sharply lower after the US Department of Agriculture raised its US harvest outlook in a monthly report, shocking most traders who had been expecting a cut.

    • The USDA estimated US corn production at 16.034 billion bushels, with an average yield of 181.2 bushels per acre. If realized, it would be the second-highest US harvest on record.

    • The forecast was up from USDA's September estimate of a 15.8-billion-bushel crop, with an average yield of 178.5 bushels per acre. Analysts expected output to decline to 15.721 billion bushels, with an average yield of 177.7 bushels per acre, according to a Reuters poll.

    • The USDA's US corn ending stocks outlook for the 2026/27 season swelled to 1.849 billion bushels, up from a forecast of 1.567 billion a month earlier and well above the average analyst estimate of 1.670 billion.

    • Farmers were actively harvesting corn this week amid mostly dry weather around the Midwest. Late-week rains stalled field work in the western Corn Belt. The USDA is due to update its estimate of harvest progress in a weekly report on Tuesday after the agency estimated 23% of the crop had been gathered as of last Sunday.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 fell by as much as the 30-cent daily trading limit and settled 20-1/2 cents lower at $4.79-3/4 a bushel.

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 9 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell on Friday after the US Department of Agriculture raised its US harvest outlook by more than expected, but settled higher after a short-covering and technical-buying bounce from six-week lows.

    • In a monthly report on Friday, the USDA raised its 2026 US soybean production outlook to 4.562 billion bushels, up from its forecast for 4.535 billion a month earlier. The agency projected the average yield at 53.1 bushels per acre, from 52.8 bpa previously. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expected production at 4.534 billion bushels with an average yield of 52.8 bpa.

    • The USDA also raised its US soybean ending stocks outlook for the 2026/27 season to 315 million bushels, from 310 million previously. Analysts, on average, had expected a cut to 305 million bushels.

    • Soybean harvesting accelerated this week amid mostly dry weather around the Midwest, although late-week rains stalled field work on parts of the western Midwest. The USDA is due to update its estimate of harvest progress in a weekly report on Tuesday after it estimated 25% of the crop had been gathered as of last Sunday.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 4-1/2 cents higher at $12.92 per bushel.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 was $8.00 higher at $365.60 per ton.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Updates for market close and trader's comment

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 9 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures slipped more than 1% on Friday, after the government did not announce an expected export duty waiver in its Budget, though the contract still posted its first weekly gain in three weeks.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for October delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange slid 69 ringgit, or 1.48%, to 4,592 ringgit ($1,124.66) a metric ton at the close. The contract gained 1.26% this week.

    Palm oil futures reversed earlier gains as optimism over a possible Malaysian export duty waiver faded after the anticipated government Budget announcement failed to materialise, said David Ng, a proprietary trader at Kuala Lumpur-based trading firm Iceberg X Sdn Bhd.

    The market had speculated that Malaysia may propose waiving export duties on crude palm oil during Friday's budget session, thus, making its exports more competitive with those of rival Indonesia. However, during the budget announcement, the government only announced support measures focused solely on replanting assistance for smallholders.

    "Looking into next week, we expect cautious trading as investors assess the upcoming Malaysian Palm Oil Board supply-demand report, particularly September inventories and export performance," he added.

    Malaysia's palm oil inventories are expected to hit an all-time high in September, topping the December 2018 peak, as production soared to record volumes, outpacing sluggish export demand, a Reuters survey showed.

    MPOB is expected to release its data on Monday.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.91%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 added 0.83%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were up 0.49%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Oil prices fell as Middle East supply concerns eased after US President Donald Trump said the country would not attack Iran before US elections next month, amid productive talks to end their war that has disrupted global energy markets. O/R

    Weaker crude oil futures make palm a less attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.12% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

  • Updates with settlement prices

    • Trump says US will not attack Iran before midterms
    • China to resume refined fuel exports, sources say
    • Brent, US crude head for weekly gain

    By Erwin Seba

    HOUSTON, Oct 9 (Reuters) - Oil prices settled higher on Friday as Hurricane Isaias churned toward the northern Gulf of Mexico and oil companies shut more than 70% of crude production in US waters.

    Earlier in the session, crude prices were down as US President Donald Trump said talks with Iran to end the war in the Middle East had been "productive." Trump also teased an upcoming, undisclosed announcement on diesel and said he was considering suspending the federal gasoline tax.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled at $104.72, up 44 cents or 0.42%. US West Texas Intermediate crude futures CLc1 finished at $91.85 a barrel, up 36 cents, or 0.39%.

    Plans by China, the world's top oil importer, to resume refined fuel exports after a brief halt during its Golden Week holiday, also weighed on the market. Reuters reported the development on Friday, citing sources.

    On a weekly basis, Brent and WTI prices are set to rise.

    Trump's Iran pledge along with China's resumption of product exports were moving prices lower, PVM Oil Associates analyst Tamas Varga said.

    Prices have been volatile this week as threats to shipping in the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz, which carried shipments equal to about 20% of global oil and fuel before the war, have increased.

    Trump said on Thursday that Washington was having "productive discussions" with Iran and said no attack was planned before the November 3 midterm congressional elections after media reports that he was considering an attack before then.

    Iran's Tasnim news agency reported the same day that Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Tehran is reviewing the US response to its proposal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days.

    The US is still pressuring Iran economically, imposing sanctions targeting individuals, networks and 17 vessels for transporting Iranian crude, oil products and petrochemicals.

    The Iran war and the conflict between Russia and Ukraine have disrupted supplies of refined fuels such as gasoline, jet fuel and especially diesel fuel.

    The oil market is also contending with Hurricane Isaias in the Gulf of Mexico where producers have shut in about 1.3 million barrels per day, or 62.9%, of current oil production as of Thursday, according to the US Marine Minerals Administration.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 9 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's dry bulk freight index fell to a more than six-week low on Friday and posted a weekly loss, as a decline in capesize rates continued to weigh on the market.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell 56 points, or 1.9%, to 2,917 points, its lowest level since August 24. The index fell 7.3% for the week.

    • The capesize index .BACI fell 172 points, or 3.8%, to 4,340 points, its lowest level since July 31. The index logged a 13.9% weekly loss.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, fell $1,560 to $35,860.

    • Iron ore prices extended their decline to multi-month lows on Friday, weighed down by lacklustre steel consumption and anticipation of rising supply of the key steelmaking ingredient.IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI gained 14 points, or 0.6%, to reach 2,424 points; it gained 2.2% this week.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, rose $125 to $21,813.

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS increased 6 points, or 0.3%, to 1,814 points and marked a weekly gain of 1.4%.
