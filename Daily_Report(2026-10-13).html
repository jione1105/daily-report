🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 12 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell on Monday under pressure from forecasts for a record-large US crop and active harvesting amid mostly dry weather around the Midwest.

    • In a monthly report on Friday, the US Department of Agriculture raised its 2026 US soybean production outlook to 4.562 billion bushels, up from its forecast for 4.535 billion a month earlier. The agency projected the average yield at 53.1 bushels per acre, from 52.8 bpa previously. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expected production at 4.534 billion bushels with an average yield of 52.8 bpa.

    • The USDA is due to release its weekly crop progress report on Tuesday, a day later than normal due to a federal holiday on Monday.

    • Soybean planting in Brazil is moving at its fastest pace ever to reach about one-fifth of projected planted area as rains favored field work, consultancy firm Patria AgroNegocios said on Friday.

    • Traders are watching for new buying demand from top importer China after a recent trade meeting between presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping. China's punitive tariff on US soybeans remains in place, which has discouraged private Chinese importers from booking US shipments.

    • China's Agriculture Ministry on Monday lowered its 2026/27 soybean output forecast by 590,000 metric tons from last month's estimate to 20.36 million tons in its October outlook, as planted area and yields missed expectations. Its soybean import forecast remained unchanged at 95.5 million tons, well below the USDA's forecast for 115 million tons.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 was $3.10 higher at $368.70 per ton.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Updates with closing prices

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 12 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures climbed 1% on Monday, rebounding from the previous session's losses, as firmer Dalian palm olein prices and strong export data lent support, though record-high September stockpiles capped gains.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for December delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange gained 46 ringgit, or 1%, to 4,638 ringgit ($1,136.49) a metric ton at the close. The contract fell 1.48% in the previous session.

    Crude palm oil prices traded higher, supported by strength in Dalian palm olein, but upside potential is capped by expectations of higher palm oil end-stocks, a Kuala Lumpur-based trader said.

    Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles in September surged to 3.51 million metric tons as production hit an all-time high, surpassing market expectations and reaching levels that were once thought impossible.

    Production climbed for a fourth straight month in September, jumping 16.48% to 2.12 million tons and eclipsing the previous high set in August 2015, while exports fell for a second straight month, sliding 12.13% to 1.12 million tons.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products for October 1-10 rose between 33.5% and 37.7% from the month earlier.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 shed 0.3%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 added 0.23%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were up 0.41%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Oil prices oscillated as the market considered possible further supplies after US President Donald Trump hailed fresh Russian diesel exports against supply-constricting signals after major attacks on Saudi Arabia. O/R

    Stronger crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.05% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    ($1 = 4.0810 ringgit)

    By Laila Kearney and Shadia Nasralla

    NEW YORK/LONDON, Oct 12 (Reuters) - Oil prices rose by nearly $2 a barrel on Monday as attacks on Saudi Arabia outweighed the prospect of increased supplies, even as US President Donald Trump hailed fresh Russian diesel exports and a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire on energy targets.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled up $1.93, or 1.8%, at $106.65 a barrel, the highest since September 15. US West Texas Intermediate crude CLc1 settled at $93.74 a barrel, rising $1.89, or 2%.

    The Iran-aligned Houthis claimed responsibility for a Saturday strike on Riyadh's international airport that killed 13 people and wounded more than 300. Further attacks happened on Sunday, intensifying conflict that could further hamper crude oil supplies from one of the world's biggest producers.

    "The shocking Houthi missile attack on Saudi Arabia’s Riyadh airport will obviously escalate the retaliation against the Houthis in Yemen," investor and analyst Louis Navellier said in a note. "Right now, Saudi Arabia’s refineries are operating, but five were temporarily closed when a pipeline to the Red Sea was closed after missile attacks."

    More attacks on tankers sailing through the Strait of Hormuz have been reported this month, threatening to dampen a recovery in Middle East crude exports seen in September.

    A tanker was struck by a drone in the vicinity of Oman's Sohar, while another was damaged transiting Hormuz, the ​United ​Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported.

    "The sense of some kind of normality of exports from the Strait of Hormuz was shattered last week. Fresh attacks by the Houthis on Saudi Arabia added to this and helped to lift prices," said SEB commodities analyst Bjarne Schieldrop.

    "Hard choices over what to defend will increasingly have to be made: oil infrastructure, military installations, royal palaces, civilian airports or other."

    Goldman Sachs, in a note, raised its Brent price forecast for December to $90 a barrel from $85, estimating a risk premium of just over $20 a barrel.

    WTI prices, they added, would likely remain low versus Brent due to high US-Europe shipping costs and average $81 in December 2026 and $73 in 2027.

    In the Gulf of Mexico, energy majors Shell SHEL.L, Chevron CVX.N and BP BP.N were seeking to redeploy personnel and restore production at their platforms after Hurricane Isaias made landfall early on Saturday.

    MORE RUSSIAN DIESEL

    ICE gasoil futures LGOc1 fell 2.3% to $1,449 per metric ton after Trump said on Friday that Russia will immediately supply more than 300,000 metric tons (2.25 million barrels) of diesel to US and global markets, with more to come later.

    "The release will be developing gradually through the rest of this year and the expected amounts during the coming month don’t appear capable of forcing much additional downside price pressure," said analyst Jim Ritterbusch of Ritterbusch and Associates in Florida, who noted that he did not expect much of a price reduction by the time the US midterm elections take place three weeks from now.

    "The immediate impact of the deal is questionable, as Russia’s refining capacity has been severely reduced," PVM analyst Tamas Varga said. "However, Moscow has partially lifted its ban on diesel exports, allowing 500,000 tons (around 3.7 million barrels) to be released onto the international market — equivalent to roughly one day of US consumption and around 13% of global demand."

    Trump also unexpectedly announced on Sunday that Ukraine and Russia had agreed to a ceasefire covering energy infrastructure. Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelenskiy accused Russia of violating the energy ceasefire on Monday, while Russia did not comment.

    The International Energy Agency's Fatih Birol said all members should assess whether additional diesel releases are necessary and that countries which have not yet fully delivered pledged volumes should accelerate releases, prioritising diesel.
