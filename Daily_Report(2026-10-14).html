🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Updates with closing prices

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 13 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures reversed earlier losses to hit a more than two-week high on Tuesday, as concerns over tighter supplies next year — due to El Niño and growing biodiesel demand — outweighed pressure from currently high stockpiles.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for December delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange rose 45 ringgit, or 0.97%, to 4,682 ringgit ($1,146.43) a metric ton, the highest closing price since September 24.

    The bearish September stocks are already reflected in the market as it had already weakened before and after the release of the Malaysian Palm Oil Board's data, said Cheang Kang Wei, agricultural commodities analyst at the Singapore-based StoneX brokerage.

    Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles in September surged to 3.51 million metric tons as production hit an all-time high, surpassing market expectations and reaching unprecedented levels.

    However, Cheang said that the key question now is whether stocks can continue rising in October, noting that inventories could still remain elevated, but the pace of the build should start slowing.

    Stock growth should begin moderating by November if exports hold above 1.5 million tons while production stabilises around 2.0 million tons, Cheang added. But if demand disappoints while production remains near record levels, stocks could stay above 3.5 million tons for longer and continue weighing on prices.

    "The market is also increasingly looking beyond the current inventory situation and focusing on potential supply risks in 2027, including the lagged impact of El Niño and Indonesia's growing biodiesel demand under B50," he said.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products for October 1-10 rose between 33.5% and 37.7% from a month earlier.

    Oil prices fell as traders assessed supply risks from attacks on Saudi Arabia while weighing the prospect of additional diesel supplies from Russia. O/R

    Weaker crude oil futures make palm a less attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 fell 0.65%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 shed 0.77%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were up 0.55%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    ($1 = 4.0840 ringgit)

    By Scott DiSavino

    Oct 13 (Reuters) - Oil prices were mixed on Tuesday as hopes the US-Russia diesel deal could free up more supplies from Moscow offset attacks in Saudi Arabia and Yemen that could result in additional supply disruptions.

    Brent futures LCOc1 settled at $105.69 per barrel, down 96 cents, or 0.9% from Monday, which was its highest settlement since September 15. US West Texas Intermediate crude futures CLc1 rose 40 cents, or 0.4%, to settle at $94.14, the highest close for WTI since September 24.

    WTI futures for calendar 2027 CLCALYZ7 rose to a record high for a 12th day in a row, reaching $84 per barrel. On Monday, Brent futures for calendar 2027 LCOCALY27 rose to a record high for a fourth day in a row, reaching $90.

    The Kremlin said it did not see any direct link between a deal on exporting Russian diesel to the US and Moscow's stance on the Ukraine peace talks.

    It was commenting on a Reuters source-based story, which reported that US President Donald Trump's envoys had told Ukrainian officials on Friday that the deal to allow Russian diesel exports was part of an attempt to draw Russia back into negotiations over a possible peace settlement in Ukraine.

    Russia was the world's third-biggest crude oil producer behind the US and Saudi Arabia in 2025, according to US energy data. Sanctions designed to keep Russian energy out of global markets have helped support oil prices since Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022.

    Any deal to end the Russia-Ukraine war could allow Russia to export more energy.

    Ukraine received no warning from Western partners before a Russian missile attack on its capital, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said on Tuesday. Trump said Washington was still sharing intelligence even as he pressures Kyiv to halt attacks on Russian refineries.

    Trump said Russian diesel was for the rest of the world, not the US. In a sign the market is uncertain about Trump's diesel deal with Russia, US diesel futures HOc1 were trading up around 5% on Tuesday.

    Diesel prices rose to record highs in many parts of the world in recent weeks due to global supply disruptions linked to Ukraine's attacks on Russian energy infrastructure and Iran's attacks on Middle Eastenergy infrastructure.

    Elsewhere, the UK's domestic spy chief said on Tuesday that Russia and Iran were stepping up hybrid warfare campaigns in Britain.

    MIDDLE EAST ATTACKS

    Yemen's Iran-allied Houthis said Saudi Arabia was attacking the Yemeni capital Sanaa on Tuesday after a Saudi-led coalition warned civilians to stay away from an oil depot on the city outskirts, part of a rapidly escalating war between the sides.

    Concerns over the vulnerability of Gulf energy infrastructure and shipping lanes remained in focus after Iran-aligned Houthis struck Riyadh's international airport over the weekend, while tanker attacks were reported near the Strait of Hormuz.

    Loud booms were heard in the vicinity of Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran, six sources told Reuters.

    Trump on Tuesday accused Yemen's Houthi militants of deliberately trying to drive up oil prices ahead of the US midterm elections and warned that he would "never forget" their attacks on Saudi Arabia, amid a widening regional conflict fueled by the US war with Iran.

    Despite ongoing disruptions of shipments through the Strait of Hormuz during the Iran war, however, oil producer group OPEC+ posted a sharp gain in production in September, the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) said on Tuesday, driven by an increase from Saudi Arabia.

    About 20% of global oil supplies passed through the Strait of Hormuz before the US and Israel attacked Iran in February. OPEC+ includes OPEC and allies like Russia.

    OPEC also lowered its forecast for 2026 oil demand growth for a sixth straight month, and raised its forecast for next year, citing the need for the world to rebuild inventories depleted by the Iran war.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 13 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, extended losses for a seventh straight session on Tuesday, weighed down by a drop in capesize rates.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell 51 points, or 1.79%, to 2,801 points, its lowest level since August 20 and its seventh consecutive session of losses.

    • The capesize index .BACI fell 154 points, or 3.71%, to 3,994 points, its lowest level since late July.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, fell $1,396 to $32,719.

    • Iron ore futures fell on Tuesday as ample global supply, elevated Chinese port inventories and declining shipping costs weighed on prices.IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI was unchanged at 2,432 points.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, were steady at $21,886.
