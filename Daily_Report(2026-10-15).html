🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 14 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures eased on Wednesday on rising supplies from the ongoing Midwest harvest and as dry weather was expected across the region following midweek rains this week.

    • Mostly dry weather around the Midwest for the past week allowed farmers to accelerate harvesting after rain delays earlier in the fall. 🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 14 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell for a third straight day on Wednesday on stronger-than-anticipated US harvest progress, although losses were limited by a resumption of Chinese purchases of US cargoes.

    • Mostly dry weather around the Midwest for the past week allowed farmers to accelerate harvesting after rain delays earlier in the fall. Midweek rains this week stalled field work but drier conditions were expected by the end of the week, forecasters said.

    • The US Department of Agriculture said that 46% of the nation's soybean crop had been harvested as of Sunday, above the average estimate for 44% complete in a Reuters poll of analysts.

    • Chinese government buyers booked 8 to 10 cargoes of US soybeans on Tuesday for shipment in December and beyond, traders told Reuters. The USDA confirmed private sales of 470,000 metric tons to China in a daily export sales announcement on Wednesday.

    • The General Administration of Customs of China said the country's soybean imports in September fell 7.7% from the same month a year earlier.

    • China's soybean crushing sector faces significant overcapacity, Lv Xiuping, a Communist Party committee member and chief accountant at state-run COFCO Oils & Fats, said at the 19th China International Oils & Oilseeds Conference on Wednesday.

    • The National Oilseed Processors Association is due to release September US crush data on Thursday. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expect NOPA to report the September crush at a one-year low of 203.737 million bushels and end-of-month soyoil stocks at 1.116 billion pounds, the lowest since November 2024.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 6-1/2 cents lower at $12.77-1/4 per bushel.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 was $1.30 higher at $364.50 per ton.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 settled 0.96 cent lower at 67.06 cents per pound.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Updates for market close

    JAKARTA, Oct 14 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures closed higher for a third straight session on Wednesday, buoyed by stronger crude oil prices and gains in rival edible oils on the Dalian market.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for December delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange gained 17 ringgit, or 0.36%, to 4,699 ringgit ($1,151.72) a metric ton at the close.

    "The FCPO is tracking Dalian performance and crude oil prices today," a Kuala Lumpur-based trader said.

    Oil prices edged higher on Wednesday after an attack on the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's state-owned oil company, while Iranian officials told Reuters they were deploying new tactics to target ships, seeking to keep oil prices high and influence the US midterm elections. O/R

    Stronger crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.71%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 gained 0.98%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were up 0.03%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Malaysia has lowered its November crude palm oil reference price, a change that maintains the export duty at 10%, a circular on the Malaysian Palm Oil Board website showed on Wednesday.

    Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles in September surged to 3.51 million metric tons as production hit an all-time high, surpassing market expectations and reaching levels that were once thought impossible.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products for October 1-10 rose between 33.5% and 37.7% from a month earlier.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.1% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.



    ($1 = 4.0800 ringgit)

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 14 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, fell to a more than two-month low on Wednesday, extending its losing streak to eight consecutive sessions weighed down by a decline in capesize rates, while panamax rates also weakened..

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell 134 points, or 4.78%, to 2,667 points, its lowest level since July 29 and its eighth consecutive session of losses.

    • The capesize index .BACI fell 364 points, or 9.11%, to 3,630 points, its lowest level since late June.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, fell $3,302 to $29,417.

    • Dalian iron ore futures fell on Wednesday as rising blast furnace maintenance in China dampened demand, while higher port inventories in Australia and Brazil pointed to ample supply.IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI lost 23 points, or 0.95%, to reach 2,409 points.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, fell $206 to $21,680.

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS edged up 12 points, or 0.65%, to 1,845 points.
