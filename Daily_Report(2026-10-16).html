🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 15 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell to a seven-week low on Thursday on harvest pressure and forecasts for dry weather in the coming week, and as the domestic soy processing pace was slower than expected last month.

    • The harvest pressure more than offset support from renewed buying by China. The Department of Agriculture on Tuesday confirmed private sales of 330,000 metric tons of US soybeans to China, a day after confirming 470,000 tons in sales.

    • The US domestic soybean crush last month fell short of expectations, according to a monthly National Oilseed Processors Association report released on Thursday, which showed the September crush at a 13-month low of 196.3 million bushels.

    • Soyoil stocks as of September 30 were at a nearly 12-year low of 1.053 billion pounds, according to NOPA, also below trade estimates.

    • A recent stretch of dry weather around the US Midwest has allowed farmers to accelerate harvesting of summer crops, and many growers were gathering soybeans before corn. Rains slowed the pace of field work in the western Midwest on Thursday, but a drier pattern was expected in the upcoming week, forecasters said.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    JAKARTA, Oct 15 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures eased on Thursday, ending a three-session winning streak, as weaker rival vegetable oils on the Dalian and Chicago markets pressured prices, despite stronger export estimates for the first half of October.

    The benchmark December palm oil contract on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange was down 78 ringgit, or 1.66%, at 4,622 ringgit ($1,132.84) a metric ton at close.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 fell 1.98%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1lost 3.03%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 slipped 0.92%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that Malaysian palm oil products exports for October 1-15 jumped between 58.3% and 43.2% from a month earlier.

    India's palm oil imports in September rose 4.5% from August to 817,884 metric tons, the Solvent Extractors' Association of India said on Thursday.

    Malaysia lowered its November crude palm oil reference price, keeping the export duty at 10%, a circular on the Malaysian Palm Oil Board website showed on Wednesday.

    Crude oil prices fell by about 2% on Thursday as potential US steps to ease restrictions on Russia's "shadow fleet" tankers soothed price concerns. Attacks on ships in the Middle East and Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, however, limited the decline.O/R

    Weaker crude oil futures make palm a less attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.02% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

  • Updates with settlement prices in paragraphs 2,3

    • EIA reports larger-than-expected US crude stocks draw

    • Ukraine resumes attacking Russian refineries

    By Georgina McCartney

    HOUSTON, Oct 15 (Reuters) - Brent crude was little changed on Thursday as investors weighed possible US steps to ease restrictions on the world’s "shadow fleet" of tankers, while conflict in the Middle East kept a geopolitical risk premium in the market.

    The international benchmark LCOc1 settled down 14 cents, or 0.13%, at $105.92 a barrel.

    US West Texas Intermediate crude futures CLc1 settled up $2.88, or 3.03%, to $97.97 a barrel, after a large US crude stock draw helped support prices.

    The shadow fleet discussions are happening days after President Donald Trump temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian diesel fuel to ease a surge in prices globally. The new talks centre on Washington potentially allowing "legal oil" to flow on previously sanctioned vessels, a US official said.

    The Department of Energy said any move to relieve sanctions on the shadow fleet would not help Russia move oil or oil products. The Treasury Department referred questions to the Department of Energy.

    US crude stocks and distillate inventories fell while gasoline inventories rose last week, the Energy Information Administration said on Thursday. EIA/S

    Crude inventories fell by 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million barrels in the week ended October 9, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a 288,000-barrel draw.

    SHIP ATTACKS SUSTAIN OIL RISK PREMIUM

    Escalating conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis and renewed attacks on ships in the region supported prices.

    A container ship and a dry bulk carrier both sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by projectiles in separate incidents, maritime officials said on Wednesday.

    Attacks around the waterway have surged over the past 10 days, with Iran saying it was stepping up strikes in response to the US blockade of its ports and tougher sanctions introduced last month.

    "The ongoing conflict between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis is keeping a geopolitical premium in oil markets, but energy infrastructure has not been attacked in the last several days," MUFG analysts said in a note.

    Houthis fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in the southern city of Aden on Thursday, killing two guards, while the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen detailed dozens of attacks on Saudi infrastructure in the escalating conflict.

    Ukraine resumed its attacks on Russian refineries with a strike on a plant in the Ryazan region, after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy voiced anger at a "dangerous" withdrawal of US intelligence.

    The International Energy Agency agreed on Thursday to release over 20 million barrels of diesel to the market as soon as possible and any oil remaining from a record emergency reserves release pledged in March to boost supply and cool soaring prices.

    In March, countries had pledged to release more than 400 million barrels of crude and oil products as prices soared.

    Spain will release 4.4 million barrels from strategic reserves in October, an Energy Ministry official said on Thursday.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 15 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, fell to its lowest level in more than three months on Thursday, weighed down by lower rates for larger vessels.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell 122 points, or 4.57%, to 2,545 points, its lowest level since June 30 and its ninth consecutive session of losses.

    • The capesize index .BACI fell 308 points, or 8.48%, to 3,322 points, its lowest level since early April.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, fell $2,792 to $26,625.

    • Iron ore prices slipped for a third straight session on Thursday to their lowest level in more than two years, weighed down by persistent concerns over weak demand in top consumer China and prospects of growing supply of the key steelmaking ingredient. IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI lost 53 points, or 2.20%, to reach 2,356 points.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, fell $480 to $21,200.

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS edged up 11 points, or 0.60%, to 1,856 points.
