🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 16 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures fell to a two-month low on Friday on technical selling and fund liquidation that dragged the market to a seventh straight weekly decline.

    • Wheat prices have remained under pressure despite a continued grain shipping disruptions on the Black Sea caused by the Russia-Ukraine war.

    • Forecasts for favorable US winter wheat planting weather in the coming week weighed on the market. Rains this week in the southern Plains provided recently planted crops ample moisture for germination, forecasters said. Drier conditions over the coming week should allow for continued planting progress, which stood at 51% complete as of last Sunday.

    • The US Department of Agriculture reported net weekly export sales of US wheat at 319,900 metric tons in the current marketing year for the week ended October 8, near the low end of a range of trade estimates.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 closed 9-1/2 cents lower at $6.60-1/2 per bushel, the lowest level since mid-August. The benchmark contract was down 1.6% in the week, its seventh consecutive weekly drop.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 last traded 9-1/4 cents lower at $7.03-3/4 a bushel.

    • Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 ended 9-1/4 cents lower at $6.76-1/2 a bushel.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 16 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures fell for a fourth straight session on Friday, nearing a two-month low, on forecasts for dry weather over the coming week that should be favorable for continued harvesting of what is expected to be a massive US crop.

    • A recent stretch of dry weather around the US Midwest has allowed farmers to accelerate harvesting of summer crops, although many growers were gathering soybeans before corn. Rains slowed the pace of field work in the western Midwest at midweek, but a drier pattern was expected over the weekend and early next week, forecasters said.

    • The US Department of Agriculture reported net weekly export sales of US corn at 1,122,400 metric tons in the current marketing year for the week ended October 8, at the high end of a range of trade estimates.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 settled 3-3/4 cents lower at $4.74-1/4 a bushel after hitting overhead technical resistance at its 200-day moving average. The benchmark contract was down 1.1% in the week, its third straight weekly decline.

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 16 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell to a fresh seven-week low on Friday on technical and fund selling and as dry weather forecast over the coming week was expected to accelerate harvesting across the Midwest.

    • The harvest pressure more than offset support from renewed buying by China. The US Department of Agriculture on Friday confirmed private sales totaling 558,000 metric tons of US soybeans to China, bringing the confirmed sales total to China to 1.358 million tons over the past three days. The USDA on Friday also reported 236,000 tons sold to undisclosed buyers, sales which traders said were likely also likely bound for China.

    • A recent stretch of dry weather around the US Midwest has allowed farmers to accelerate harvesting of summer crops, and many growers were gathering soybeans before corn. Midweek rains this week stalled field work, but drier weather is expected through the weekend and early next week, forecasters said.

    • The USDA reported net weekly export sales of US soybeans at 574,000 metric tons in the current marketing year for the week ended October 8, at the low end of a range of trade estimates.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 1-3/4 cents lower at $12.61-1/4 per bushel after earlier touching the lowest level since August 27. The benchmark contract was down 2.4% in the week.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    JAKARTA, Oct 16 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures climbed on Friday, supported by bargain buying and strong export demand as cargo surveyors reported higher shipments in the first half of October.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange gained 44 ringgit, or 0.93%, to 4,769 ringgit ($1,169.16) a metric ton at the close. The futures posted a 3.85% weekly gain.

    India's palm oil imports in September jumped to a seven-month high as refiners favoured it over sunflower oil.

    "The CPO futures were seen trading higher today on some bargain buying. Additionally, stronger Malaysian palm oil export for first half of October has also helped," said Anilkumar Bagani, research head of Mumbai-based vegetable oils broker Sunvin Group.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products during October 1-15 jumped between 43.2% and 58.3% from a month earlier.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 fell 0.86%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 slipped 0.96%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 0.54%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, weakened 0.05% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly cheaper for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    Malaysia lowered its November crude palm oil reference price, keeping the export duty at 10%, a circular on the Malaysian Palm Oil Board website showed on Wednesday.

    Crude oil prices eased on Friday but were heading for weekly gains on uncertainty over Middle East supplies and a larger-than-expected draw from US crude stockpiles. European diesel margins dropped following the IEA's decision to release more stock. O/R

    Weaker crude oil futures make palm a less attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    By Arathy Somasekhar

    HOUSTON, Oct 16 (Reuters) - Brent touched $110 a barrel for the first time since May on Friday on worries that escalating tensions in the Middle East could further tighten supplies, while investors assessed how much diesel Russia could supply to the global market.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled $3.94, or 3.7%, higher at $109.86, the highest since May 19, while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures CLc1 closed $3.44, or 3.5%, higher at $101.41, the highest since September 17.

    So far this week, Brent has risen 4.9% and WTI 10.4%.

    "WTI and Brent reversed earlier losses as escalation fears resurfaced amid rumours of attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure," research and consultancy Energy Aspects said in a note.

    "Israeli reports suggest Iran may be preparing a pre-emptive strike following unusual military movements detected by Western intelligence. Although unverified, these developments reinforce our view that persistent escalation risks will keep flat prices supported," Energy Aspects added.

    A Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile launched towards the Red Sea city of Yanbu. The port is critical for Saudi Arabia's efforts to bypass the Strait of Hormuz and keep oil flowing to international markets.

    Initial reports indicated no damage to Yanbu's infrastructure, consultants Rapidan Energy Group said.

    Supply concerns also increased as attacks around the Strait of Hormuz surged over the past 10 days, with Iran saying it was stepping up strikes in response to the US blockade of its ports and tougher sanctions introduced last month.

    "Intensified Iranian attacks drove Gulf crude flows sharply lower," Kpler analysts said in a report on Thursday, adding that they expected a slower and more uneven recovery in Hormuz flows in the absence of a comprehensive political deal.

    RUSSIA REFINERY ATTACKS

    Ukraine struck a Russian oil refinery on Thursday after President Volodymyr Zelenskiy declared that Kyiv was abandoning a ceasefire covering energy sites announced by US President Donald Trump and criticized Trump's willingness to strike deals with Moscow.

    Russia's Ryazan oil refinery has suspended operations at a primary crude distillation unit following a drone attack in the region on Thursday, two industry sources told Reuters.

    "Conflict in the Middle East still keeps coming back and I think it's questionable how much diesel fuel Russia can actually deliver to the markets. Those two factors are just continuing to have traders be buyers," said Dennis Kissler, senior vice president of trading at BOK Financial.

    To boost supply, the International Energy Agency agreed on Thursday to release more than 20 million barrels of fuel to the market.

    The United States also issued a request for proposal for an exchange of up to 17 million barrels of crude oil from the nation's Strategic Petroleum Reserve, the Department of Energy said on Friday.

    Washington previously agreed to loan 172 million barrels of oil from the SPR as part of a wider deal with about 30 countries in the International Energy Agency to release 400 million barrels from reserves around the world.

    Brent crude was little changed on Thursday, while WTI settled up more than 3% after news that the US was weighing plans to ease restrictions on the world’s "shadow fleet" of tankers and after a big US crude stock draw on hurricane production shut-ins.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 16 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, fell on Friday, extending losses to a third straight week as weakness in capesize vessel rates continued to weigh on the market.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell 34 points, or 1.34%, to 2,511 points, its lowest level since June 30. The index declined about 14% for the week.

    • The capesize index .BACI fell 73 points, or 2.20%, to 3,249 points, its lowest level since early April and posted a weekly decline of 25%.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, fell $664 to $25,961.

    • Iron ore prices rebounded on Friday after touching more than two-year lows the day before, aided by dip buying amid signs of improving steel margins and downstream consumption in top consumer China.IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI lost 42 points, or 1.78%, to reach 2,314 points.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, fell $373 to $20,827.

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS edged up 9 points, or 0.48%, to 1,865 points.
