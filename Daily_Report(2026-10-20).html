🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 19 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures fell to a more than two-month low on Monday on fund long liquidation and spillover pressure from lower corn and soybeans.

    • Wheat traders continued to assess disruptions to grain exports from the Black Sea region due to Russia's war against Ukraine.

    • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy asked neighboursPoland, Romania, Slovakia and Hungary to allow the transit of Ukrainian grain cargoes because of a Black Sea blockade, Hungary's prime minister said over the weekend.

    • Russian wheat export prices declined slightly last week, while Ukraine's weekly exports of wheat, corn and barley in the period from October 8 to 14 rose by 29% from the previous week to 373,000 metric tons, analysts said.

    • The US Department of Agriculture is due to update its weekly crop progress estimates later on Monday. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expect the agency to report that US farmers have planted 65% of their winter wheat crop as of Sunday.

    • The USDA said 240,308 metric tons of US wheat was inspected for export in the week ended October 15, down from 307,025 tons a week earlier and below trade estimates that ranged from 300,000 to 400,000 tons.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 closed 5-1/4 cents lower at $6.55-1/4 per bushel, the lowest level since August 12.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 last traded 4-1/2 cents lower at $6.99-1/2 a bushel.

    • Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 was 6-1/4 cents lower at $6.70-1/4 a bushel.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 19 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures fell for a fifth straight session on Monday and hit its lowest point in more than two months as dry Midwest weather boosted harvest progress while forecasts called for continued good weather through the end of the week.

    • The US Department of Agriculture is due to update its weekly crop progress estimates later on Monday. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expect the agency to report that US farmers have harvested 49% of their corn crop as of Sunday.

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 19 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell to a 7-1/2-week low on Monday on technical selling and fund long liquidation as dry weather across the Midwest accelerated harvesting of what is forecast to be a record-large US crop.

    • Scattered rains last week stalled field work in parts of the Midwest, but drier weather was expected through the end of the week, forecasters said.

    • The US Department of Agriculture is due to update its weekly crop progress estimates later on Monday. Analysts polled by Reuters, on average, expect the agency to report that US farmers have harvested 66% of their soybean crop as of Sunday.

    • The harvest pressure more than offset support from renewed buying by China. The US Department of Agriculture on Monday confirmed private sales totaling 114,000 metric tons of US soybeans to China, bringing the confirmed sales total to China to 1.472 million tons over the past four days. The USDA on Monday also reported 261,000 tons sold to undisclosed buyers, sales which traders said were also likely bound for China.

    • The USDA said 2,001,750 metric tons of US soybeans were inspected for export in the week ended October 15, up from 1,109,385 tons a week earlier and above trade estimates that ranged from 1.2 million to 1.6 million tons.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 7-3/4 cents lower at $12.53-1/2 per bushel after earlier reaching their lowest point since August 27.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 was $2.30 lower at $354.80 per ton.

    JAKARTA, Oct 19 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures retreated on Monday, weighed down by weakness in Chicago soyoil and elevated stockpiles, while stronger edible oils on the Dalian exchange capped losses.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange fell 3 ringgit, or 0.06%, to 4,766 ringgit ($1,168.14) a metric ton at the close.

    "The mixed performance of Dalian palm olein and Chicago soybean oil futures prompted cautious trading. Meanwhile, upside potential remain limited by Malaysia’s elevated palm oil inventories," a Kuala Lumpur-based trader said.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.4%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 gained 0.58%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOc2 were down 0.09%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Another trader said the contract was also pressured by market speculation around Malaysia weighing the waiver of export taxes on crude palm oil to boost shipments and reduce high stockpiles.

    Malaysia's agricultural exports were down 5.7% in September, amid declining shipments of palm oil and palm oil-based products, government data showed on Monday.

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

  • Updates with settlement prices, adds details on December US contract

    • Kpler says Gulf crude flows fell to an average of about 8.5 million bpd last week
    • Tanker hit mine and exploded in the southern Strait of Hormuz, IRGC says
    • Houthis claim attacks on Saudi energy, military infrastructure, including Aramco facility

    By Siddharth Cavale

    NEW YORK, Oct 19 (Reuters) - Oil prices were mixed on Monday after attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis on a Yemeni military training camp and renewed attacks on Saudi energy, airport and military facilities.

    Brent crude futures settled 31 cents, or 0.3% lower, at $109.55 a barrel, after falling more than $3 earlier in the session as investors booked profits following last week's rally to a five-month high.

    US West Texas Intermediate gained $2.25, or 2.2%, to $103.66 a barrel, the highest settlement since September 15. However, that benchmark's trade was relatively thin ahead of the October 20 expiry of the front-month contract.

    Trading activity was concentrated in the more liquid December contract, with more than 195,000 lots changing hands, compared with about 26,000 lots in the expiring November contract. The December contract settled at $98.54, down 0.5%.

    Yemen's Houthis said on Monday they had carried out attacks on Saudi energy, airport and military targets, including an Aramco facility in Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield, King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Abha airport. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the claims and Reuters could not independently verify them.

    Separately, an oil tanker caught fire and exploded after striking a naval mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said, according to Iranian media.

    Crude flows through the region fell to a seven-day average of about 8.5 million barrels per day last week, down from more than 20 million bpd the previous week, analytics firm Kpler said, due to the increased threats to commercial shipping. Kpler reported at least 10 confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels during the week.

    SEB analyst Bjarne Schieldrop predicted increases in export volumes through Hormuz may prove short-lived as Iran could escalate attacks ahead of the November 3 US midterm elections.

    Egypt said it stood ready to support Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, though Egypt's parliament delayed until October 26 a vote that would authorize the armed forces to assist the kingdom if requested.

    Earlier on Monday, 53 people were killed in a Houthi attack and 200 were injured in an attack on a Yemeni government security force training camp in the energy-producing province of Shabwa, in coastal south-central Yemen.

    US President Donald Trump on Friday signed an executive order waiving some sanctions and tariffs related to Russian energy exports, drawing criticism from both political foes and allies, as he tries to lower energy prices ahead of the November elections.

    "The waiver raises the prospect of additional Russian fuel exports, but uncertainty over its implementation and duration limits confidence in a sustained supply increase," Gelber and Associates wrote in a note.

    Priyanka Sachdeva, head of market insight at Phillip Nova, said the waiver did little to address the physical supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, where oil tanker attacks continue.

    Despite international efforts to curb energy flows from Iran and Russia, both countries have continued exporting fuel, which has limited the effects of supply disruptions on global markets.

    Oil output from the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries rose in September, a Reuters survey found, led by gains in Saudi Arabia and Iraq.
