🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 20 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures fell to the lowest level in more than three months on Tuesday on technical selling and fund liquidation, as well as spillover pressure from lower corn and soybeans.

    • US winter wheat plantings were 63% complete as of Sunday, compared with the five-year average of 69%, the US Department of Agriculture said, after trading ended on Monday. Analysts expected 65%, according to a Reuters poll.

    • Wheat traders continued to assess disruptions to grain exports from the Black Sea region due to Russia's war against Ukraine.

    • Despite the war's disruption to Black Sea trade, Russian and Ukrainian wheat shipments continued via alternate routes, while Australia's harvest and Argentina's upcoming wheat supplies have given buyers ample options.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 closed 12 cents lower at $6.43-1/4 per bushel after hitting its lowest point since July 10. Selling accelerated as the benchmark contract broke through technical chart support at its 200-day moving average.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 last traded 9-1/2 cents lower at $6.90 a bushel.

    • Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 ended 7-3/4 cents lower at $6.61-3/4 a bushel.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 20 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures dropped for a sixth straight session on Tuesday, hitting its lowest level in 2-1/2 months, as dry Midwest weather accelerated harvesting of what is expected to be the second-largest US crop on record.

    • Fund long liquidation added further pressure.

    • Weather conditions will be favorable for harvesting in the Midwest through at least Friday, Commodity Weather Group said.

    • The US Department of Agriculture said 44% of the US corn crop was harvested as of Sunday, up from 33% a week earlier but short of the five-year average of 52%. The agency left its corn condition rating unchanged at 56% good to excellent.

    • President Donald Trump said on Monday the US Department of Agriculture will launch what he called a new "harvest fuel relief program" that will provide farmers with $8 billion in support to offset diesel costs during harvest season.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 settled 5 cents lower at $4.62 a bushel.


    (Reporting by Karl Plume; editing by David Gaffen)

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 20 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures fell to the lowest point in nearly two months on Tuesday, pressured by fund long liquidation as dry weather across the Midwest accelerated harvesting of what is forecast to be a record-large US crop.

    • The US Department of Agriculture said 64% of the US soybean crop was harvested as of Sunday, slightly below the five-year average of 65%.

    • The harvest pressure more than offset support from renewed buying by China. The US Department of Agriculture on Tuesday confirmed private sales totaling 612,000 metric tons of US soybeans to China, bringing the confirmed sales total to China to more than 2 million tons over the past five days.

    • President Donald Trump said on Monday the US Department of Agriculture will launch what he called a new "harvest fuel relief program" that will provide farmers with $8 billion in support to offset diesel costs during the harvest season.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 5-1/4 cents lower at $12.48-1/4 per bushel after earlier reaching their lowest point since August 26.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 was $6.10 lower at $348.70 per ton.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 rose 0.04 cent to settle at 66.73 cents per pound.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update with closing prices

    JAKARTA, Oct 20 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures rose to their highest closing in four weeks on Tuesday, underpinned by strength in rival vegetable oils on China's Dalian Commodity Exchange and Chicago Board of trade, as well as market talk that the Malaysian government is discussing a possible waiver of crude palm oil (CPO) export tax.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange added 52 ringgit, 1,09%, to 4,818 ringgit ($1,180.45) a metric ton at closing.

    "Strength in nearby contract months is supported by circulating market talk that the Malaysian government is discussing a possible waiver of the CPO export tax," a Kuala Lumpur-based trader said.

    "Export tax waiver could support market, as traders anticipated improved export competitiveness and stronger overseas demand, although the price impact will depend on actual export uptake and end-stock levels," the trader added.

    Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles in September surged to 3.51 million metric tons, as production hit an all-time high, the Malaysian Palm Oil Board said earlier this month.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.28%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 climbed 0.98%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOc2 were up 0.67%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.
