🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    JAKARTA, Oct 23 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures closed higher on Friday, supported by gains in Dalian soyoil and firmer crude oil prices, while posting a third straight weekly gain.

    The benchmark January palm oil contract on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange rose 25 ringgit, or 0.52%, to 4,817 ringgit ($1,179.77) a metric ton at the close, bringing its weekly gain to 1.01%.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 2.04%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 lost 0.19%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 0.07%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Oil prices rose on Friday, with Brent crude touching its highest in more than five months, after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia stoked concern over further disruption to energy supplies from the Middle East.O/R

    Firmer crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit, palm's currency of trade, also weakened 0.05% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly cheaper for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    Brazil's soybean exports are expected to fall 0.8% to 113 million metric tons in 2027 a year earlier, crushers group Abiove said on Thursday, as stronger domestic processing demand limits supply to importers such as China.

    Crude palm oil prices are expected to stay above 4,600 ringgit ($1,128) per metric ton in November on stronger export demand, sustained biodiesel consumption and a seasonal decline in production, the Malaysian Palm Oil Council said on Thursday.

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

    By Nicole Jao

    NEW YORK, Oct 23 (Reuters) - Oil prices settled more than $3 higher on Friday, their highest in nearly six months after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia stoked concerns over further disruption to energy supplies from the Middle East, where UAE authorities also reported an oil spill.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled at $116.72 a barrel, up $3.55 or 3.14%, their highest since April 29. US West Texas Intermediate crude CLc1 closed at $103.27 a barrel, up $3.80 or 3.8%.

    Brent finished the week up 6% and WTI ended 2.3% higher.

    "Fears of supply disruptions drove the advance after Iranian-backed Houthi rebels carried out a series of attacks across Saudi Arabia, including on energy infrastructure," said Adam Turnquist, chief cross-asset strategist for LPL Financial.

    On Thursday, Saudi Aramco 2222.SEordered the evacuation of its headquarters in Dhahran on the Gulf near Bahrain, Reuters reported. Fighting intensified between Saudi Arabia, the world's largest crude exporter and Yemen-based Houthis.

    An oil spill from a tanker was detected off the UAE coast of Umm Al Quwain, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said. It did not say what caused the spill and authorities have not identified the tanker involved.

    ATTACKS ON VESSELS

    Shipping sources said on October 9 that the Gem No. 2 supertanker was struck by an unidentified projectile 13 nautical miles (24 km) west of Al Jazeera in the UAE.

    "The market is concerned about these continued Iranian attacks on tankers that we've seen increase over the last week," said Andrew Lipow, president of Lipow Oil Associates. "While there has been an increase in oil transiting out of the Strait of Hormuz, that could change at any minute."

    Clashes between the Houthis and a Saudi-led coalition that backs Yemen's internationally recognised government have raged since the Iran-aligned Houthis pushed towards Bab el-Mandeb Strait last month, a shipping ​chokepoint at the mouth of the Red Sea.

    October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways, according to data compiled by a Washington-based think tank that has tracked maritime security incidents worldwide since 2017.

    President Donald Trump said on Friday the US could end the war with Iran quickly, but said perhaps Washington should allow the conflict to gradually wind down.

    STRATEGIC RESERVE RELEASES

    The US Department of Energy said it had authorized an emergency exchange of up to 400,000 barrels of crude oil from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to address a supply disruption affecting Phillips 66's Lake Charles refinery in Louisiana.

    The SPR is already at the lowest level since 1982 after President Donald Trump tapped it as the US war on Iran disrupted supply lines. Trump's predecessor, President Joe Biden, also tapped the SPR following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

    Elsewhere, Germany's national oil stockpiling agency EBV circulated a tender to sell diesel, heating oil and crude oil volumes from strategic reserves.

    This marks Germany's first oil releases from strategic reserves since G7 countries, under pressure from Trump, agreed to do so at the start of October.

    On Thursday, the head of Libya's National Oil Corp told Reuters that an oil pipeline valve from the Hamada and Tahara oilfields to Zawiya port had been shut, leading to a reduction in production at both fields.

    Ship tracking data showed the first Russian diesel bound for the US since Moscow and Washington reached an export agreement was aboard a tanker heading for New York.

    Trump announced the deal this month as part of efforts to bring down soaring diesel prices ahead of US midterm elections on November 3.

    US energy firms this week kept the total rig count unchanged for a second week in a row, energy services firm Baker Hughes BKR.O said in its closely followed report on Friday. The total oil and gas rig count, an early indicator of future output, held steady at 603 during the week ended October 23.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • By Vishak Mathew

    Oct 23 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, fell on Friday but posted a second consecutive weekly gain, supported by stronger capesize rates.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, fell by 33 points, or 1.23%, to 2,660 points. Still, the index rose about 5.9% for the week.

    • The capesize index .BACI shed 57 points, or 1.48%, to 3,791 points. But it also recorded a second consecutive weekly gain, up 16.7%.

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes including iron ore and coal, decreased by $519 to $30,877

    • Iron ore futures rose on Friday, supported by reduced Chinese port inventories, though the contract posted a seventh straight weekly loss as traders weighed weak broader demand. IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI fell 42 points, or 1.95%, to 2,109 points, its lowest level since August 24. The index declined 8.9% for the week.

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, fell by $377 to $18,981

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS dropped 3 points, or 0.16%, to 1,887 points. It was up 1.18% this week.
