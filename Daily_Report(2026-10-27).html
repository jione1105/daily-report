🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 26 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures turned lower on Monday amid new proposals to reopen Black Sea grain shipping, market analysts said.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 26 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures touched multi-month lows on Monday on pressure from the advancing US harvest, which could further bolster large stocks and increase world export supplies, and easing crude oil prices, market analysts said.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 settled 1 cent lower at $4.55 a bushel.

    • Earlier in the session, the most-active corn contract on a continuous basis Cv1 dipped to $4.50-1/4 a bushel, the lowest price seen since July 10.

    • Spillover pressure from the CBOT soybean market also weighed on prices.

    • The Brazilian real BRBYweakened around 12% against the US dollar in spot trading as trading in Brazil got under way on Monday, and the country's stock exchange halted trading via a circuit breaker safety mechanism, in the wake of President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's reelection — undoing bullish investor sentiment after his right-wing rival led the first round of voting earlier in the month.

    • Spot basis bids for corn were mixed on Monday, as the corn harvest in the US Midwest continued.

    • Oil prices inched higher after falling more than $2 a barrel on Monday, as persistent attacks on key producer Saudi Arabia kept investors wary and prices near six-month highs, even as diplomatic efforts to resolve the conflict in the Middle East resumed.

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 26 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures slumped sharply on Monday, at one point dropping more than 2%, as prices were weighed down by currency pressure and supplies from a record US harvest entering the market, market analysts said.

    • The Brazilian real BRBYweakened around 12% against the US dollar in spot trading as trading in Brazil got underway on Monday, and the country's stock exchange halted trading via a circuit breaker safety mechanism.

    • That came in the wake of President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's reelection, undoing bullish investor sentiment after his right-wing rival led the first round of voting earlier in the month.

    • Traders said that the surprising comeback was particularly bearish for US soybean futures, as Lula's past efforts to bolster China's purchasing of Brazilian soybeans had shown.

    • The weakness in the real also weighed on expectations for US soybean export demand, as a cheaper local currency can make Brazilian exports less expensive for global buyers, giving them a competitive edge over US supplies.

    • Earlier in the trading session, the most-active CBOT soybean contract on a continuous basis Sv1 at one point dropped to $12.27-1/2 a bushel, the lowest price since August 25.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 15-1/4 cents lower at $12.41-1/4 per bushel.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 ended $5.90 lower at $340.20 per ton.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 rose 0.18 cent to settle at 67.01 cents per pound.

    JAKARTA, Oct 26 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures slipped on Monday, pressured by declines in Dalian palm olein and Chicago soyoil, with weaker crude oil prices adding to the downside as traders awaited confirmation of Malaysia's proposed export duty waiver.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange lost 25 ringgit, or 0.52%, to 4,793 ringgit ($1,173.60) a metric ton at the close.

    "The futures opened gap lower following weakness in energy prices and in related vegetable oil futures," said Anilkumar Bagani, head of research at Mumbai-based vegetable oil broker Sunvin Group.

    "However, the main catalyst is the absence of any confirmation of an export duty waiver from Malaysia and (the) market (is) seen erasing the news premium from the palm oil prices."

    Dalian's most active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.13%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 dropped 0.52%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 0.43%.

    Palm oil tracks rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    By Siddharth Cavale

    NEW YORK, Oct 26 (Reuters) - Brent crude futures finished lower on Monday amid hopes of easing hostilities inthe Middle East, but persistent attacks on key producer Saudi Arabia and maritime shipping kept investors wary and prices hovering near six-month highs.

    Brent LCOc1 finished the day down 91 cents, or 0.8%, at $115.81 a barrel while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures CLc1 closed down $1.51, or 1.46%, at $101.76 a barrel.

    Both benchmarks retreated after strong gains on Friday, when Brent settled more than $3 higher to post a weekly gain of 6%, while WTI rose 2.3% for the week.

    Investor focus remained on tensions in the Middle East after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said they struck pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West crude oil pipeline and Riyadh airport over the weekend. There was no immediate Saudi confirmation of the Houthi attacks.

    The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen, however, said on Monday it had destroyed three drones targeting Riyadh and the kingdom's southern regions.

    Hopes for a diplomatic breakthrough in the Middle East conflict, which began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran and later spread to Saudi Arabia and Yemen, weighed on oil prices earlier in the session, with Brent falling more than $2 a barrel.

    Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran on Sunday in coordination with the United States to seek ways to ease regional tensions, according to an official briefed on the matter.

    Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistanheld talks with Iranian officials in Tehran on Monday as part of efforts to prevent further escalation in the region.

    The talks were the first high-level contact between Iran and the three nations, which signed a mutual defence pact in August.

    At the conclusion, the four countries agreed to continue talks with a view to strengthening cooperation in peace and security, Iranian state media reported.

    Egypt's parliament authorized President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi to take "all necessary measures" to help Saudi Arabia defend itself, raising the prospect of Egyptian military support for the kingdom.

    WORST MONTH FOR MARITIME ATTACKS

    The effects of the US-Israeli war with Iran that began on February 28 continue to reverberate as companies adjust to the disruptions of commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb Strait caused by the conflict.

    October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways, according to data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, a think tank that has tracked maritime security incidents worldwide since 2017.

    An oil spill from a strike-damaged tanker off the coast of the United Arab Emirates has widened, satellite images showed, triggering more beach closures and raising environmental concerns.

    The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency said on Monday it had received third-party reports of a vessel on fire near Al-Faw, Iraq, adding that the crew's status, extent of any damage and any environmental impact were unknown.

    Elsewhere, Russian refiners are raising diesel production in October after the government granted export quotas following the Kremlin's supply agreement with the United States, two industry people close to the matter said.

    Energy ministers from Saudi Arabia and Singapore said state-owned oil company Saudi Aramco would start storing crude oil in Singapore from the second half of 2027, to make Aramco's supply chain in the region more resilient.

    The head of the International Energy Agency, Fatih Birol, reiterated supply concerns, saying on Monday at an industry event in Singapore that he did not expect energy markets in 2027 to return to where they were before the Iran war.

    In the US, stocks of crude oil in the Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to 282.6 million barrels last week, the lowest level since October 1982, the Department of Energy said on Monday.
