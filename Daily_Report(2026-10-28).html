🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 27 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures fell nearly 2% on Tuesday, extending losses for a second consecutive session, as weaker Dalian oils and high stockpiles pressured the market.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange slid 87 ringgit, or 1.82%, to 4,702 ringgit ($1,151.04) a metric ton at the close.

    The market is tracking the weakness in Dalian, said a Kuala Lumpur-based trader.

    Meanwhile, LSEG Agriculture Research in its October note said that palm oil prices strengthened in October, supported by continued concerns over El Nino-related production risks and stronger domestic demand from Indonesia's biodiesel programme.
    The report, however, said that record-high Malaysian inventories would likely limit further gains.

    Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles in September surged to 3.51 million metric tons as production hit an all-time high, surpassing market expectations and reaching unprecedented levels.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 fell 2.62%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 shed 3.54%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 0.42%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Oil prices turned positive after a fall of about 1%, earlier in the session, as the chance of talks in Tehran eased fears of a broader Middle East supply shock, while renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia highlighted persistent risks. O/R

    Stronger crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, weakened 0.05% against the dollar, making the commodity slightly cheaper for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products for October 1-25 rose between 46.9% and 67.7% from a month earlier.

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

  • Updates with settlement prices

    • Saudi, Turkey, Pakistan and Iran agree to continue talks
    • October attacks on commercial shipping near Hormuz were highest since 2017
    • Brent remains near six-month peaks

    By Georgina McCartney

    HOUSTON, Oct 27 (Reuters) - Oil futures settled down by almost $2 a barrel on Tuesday, though prices remain near six-month highs due to the disruption of shipping around the Middle East, and an agreement to end the US-Israeli war with Iran remains elusive.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled down $1.93, or 1.67%, at $113.88 a barrel. US West Texas Intermediate crude CLc1 settled down $1.86, or 1.83%, at $99.90.

    Iranian Security Council chief Mohsen Rezaei met Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei on Tuesday, a day after holding talks with representatives of Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan.

    State media said Khamenei had emphasized the importance of pursuing Iran's interests and maintaining its conditions for ending the war.

    The four countries agreed to continue talks with a view to strengthening cooperation in peace and security, Iranian state media said.

    The outlook for peace remains uncertain as Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis renewed attacks on key oil producer Saudi Arabia. The Houthis said on Tuesday that they had struck Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, the ⁠biggest in the kingdom, with a ballistic missile.

    "Continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and the expanding military presence in the Gulf are keeping supply risks in place, even as diplomatic headlines encourage some of the recent premium to unwind," said Gelber & Associates analysts in a note.

    October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waterways, according to data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, a think tank that has tracked maritime security incidents worldwide since 2017.

    Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has risen but the number of vessel attacks has increased, with risks likely to become more acute if US naval support wavers, UBS said.

    Brent prices surged to more than $117 a barrel last week, their highest since April, after a wave of Iranian attacks on tankers around the strait.

    UBS said it expects Brent prices to stay elevated near $110 a barrel through the end of the year, an increase from its previous forecast for $100 a barrel at the end of the year.

    "The impression now is that there is a significant risk premium in crude oil prices for the risk that the US/Israel will attack Iran in the coming weeks," said SEB analyst Bjarne Schieldrop.

    Investors were also looking for cues from Israel's election on Tuesday, which could spell the end of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's time in office, though polling suggested results would be close and that his Likud party could still prevail.

    "The outcome of today’s parliamentary elections in Israel could also be significant for further developments in the war with Iran, as Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu, if re-elected, might attempt to achieve regime change in Iran," Commerzbank analysts wrote.

    Crude oil exports from Russia's Baltic and Black Sea ports were forecast to fall next month as domestic refiners plan to increase their output after the end of maintenance and to produce more diesel to meet the demands of a deal struck this month between Washington and Moscow, two traders said.

    Russian oil refineries increased crude processing by 25% in the second half of October compared to the beginning of the month after persistent Ukrainian drone attacks on the plants ceased, three people in the industry told Reuters.
