🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 28 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures continued to slide on Wednesday — ending lower for the ninth time in the past 10 trading sessions — on news that Russian authorities issued permits for vessels carrying grain for export to leave the Sea of Azov for the first time since July 10.

    • Russia closed maritime traffic in the area due to Ukrainian drone attacks. The permits to pass through the Kerch Strait, which links the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea, would be issued for a period through November 15, sources told Reuters.

    • Two of the sources said the vessels would be accompanied through the Black Sea by a Russian naval convoy.

    • Russia is also starting grain exports from the Arctic port of Murmansk.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 settled down 11-1/4 cents at $5.99 a bushel. Earlier in the session, the most-active wheat contract Wv1 dipped to $5.93-1/4 a bushel, the lowest since July 1.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 settled down 7-1/2 cents at $6.43 a bushel.

    • Minneapolis spring wheat futures set fresh contract lows on the day, with December spring wheat MWEZ26 settling 8-3/4 cents lower at $6.19-3/4 a bushel.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 28 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures firmed on bargain buying on Wednesday, but ample US harvest supplies continued to weigh on prices and kept the most-active contract Cv1 rangebound, market analysts said.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 settled 3 cents higher at $4.51-3/4 a bushel.

    • Traders said they continued to be worried that currency pressures will weigh on US export demand for grains this fall, as farmers begin to wind down the autumn harvest in parts of the eastern US Midwest.

    • However, the US dollar pared gains against the Japanese yen on Wednesday afternoon, after the Federal Reserve held interest rates steady. The US central bank opted to delay any changes to borrowing costs until after next week's US midterm elections and while awaiting further data on the path of inflation.

    • A weaker US dollar can improve demand for US grains by making those supplies less expensive for overseas buyers.

    • Basis bids for corn were mostly steady to stronger in the US Midwest on Wednesday as the harvest wound down in some areas, prompting some grain handlers to sweeten their bids to source fresh supplies.


    (Reporting by P.J. Huffstutter in Chicago
    Editing by Matthew Lewis)

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 28 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures Sv1 turned lower for a third straight session on Wednesday, amid fund liquidation and concern that a revival of Black Sea maritime traffic could erode export demand for US animal feeds and edible oils, market analysts said.

    • Soybeans followed wheat futures lower on news that Russian authorities issued permits for vessels carrying grain for export to leave the Sea of Azov for the first time since July 10.

    • Russia closed maritime traffic in the area due to Ukrainian drone attacks. The permits to pass through the Kerch Strait, which links the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea, would be issued for a period through November 15, sources told Reuters. Two of the sources said the vessels would be accompanied through the Black Sea by a Russian naval convoy.

    • Soyoil prices BOv1 also slumped sharply on the news, with the most-active contract at one point touching prices not seen since early July.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 closed 4-3/4 cents lower at $12.24-1/2 per bushel, while the most-active January contract SF27 ended down 5 cents at $12.38 a bushel.

    • CBOT soymeal futures were mixed. CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 ended $1.10 higher at $335.70 per ton.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 fell 1.15 cents to settle at 65.56 cents per pound.

    • Exporters sold 120,000 metric tons of US soybeans to unknown destinations for delivery in the 2026/2027 marketing year, the US Department of Agriculture said in its daily reporting system on Wednesday.

    • China's Sinograin said on Wednesday it would auction 402,000 metric tons of imported soybeans on November 3.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 28 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures gave up early gains to close lower on Wednesday, their third consecutive daily loss, despite strong export demand ahead of Diwali festive-season buying in India.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange fell 14 ringgit, or 0.3%, to 4,687 ringgit ($1,149.90) a metric ton at the close.

    The market traded higher in the morning session on the recent robust export strength seen in recent weeks amid the upcoming Diwali season, said David Ng, a proprietary trader at Kuala Lumpur-based trading firm Iceberg X Sdn Bhd.

    Cargo surveyors estimated that exports of Malaysian palm oil products for October 1-25 rose between 46.9% and 67.7% from a month earlier.

    Oil prices rose over 1% after an attack on a refinery in Saudi Arabia, the world's top crude exporter, renewed concerns about supply risks because of conflicts in the Middle East. O/R

    Stronger crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.65%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 shed 0.4%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 0.24%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    European Union soybean imports for the 2026/27 season that began in July reached 3.8 million metric tons by October 25, down 2% from the same period a year earlier, while palm oil imports were down 21% to 0.8 million tons, European Commission data showed.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.32% against the dollar, making the commodity more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    ($1 = 4.0760 ringgit)

    By Scott DiSavino

    NEW YORK, Oct 28 (Reuters) - Oil prices edged up about 1% on Wednesday on renewed concerns about additional energy supply disruptions in the Middle East due to continued fighting in Yemen, an attack on a refinery in Saudi Arabia and worries the US could strike Iran after next week's US midterm elections.

    Brent futures LCOc1 rose $1.44, or 1%, to settle at $115.32 per barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude CLc1 rose $2.88, or 3%, to settle at $102.78.

    That price gain pushed Brent back into overbought territory for the fourth time in five days.

    Yemen's Houthis attacked Aden airport on Wednesday, Yemeni government forces said, after the Iran-aligned group warned people to evacuate the facility as fighting escalated in the impoverished country caught in the wider Middle East conflict.

    On Tuesday, the Houthis said they had hit Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery with a missile, intensifying their strikes against the world's second-largest crude producer behind the US. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the strike on the 550,000-barrel-per-day facility.

    "Crude futures once again pricing in nearer term tighter supplies on news the US is likely to re-start military operations soon, which could be more intense than in the past given the latest attacks on Saudi Arabia and other Middle East allies," Dennis Kissler, senior vice president of trading at US financial services firm BOK Financial, said in a note.

    In recent weeks, several analysts have said renewed US strikes on Iran could happen sometime after the US midterm elections on November 3.

    In hopes of ending the now eight-month-long conflict, Qatari mediators have presented Iran with a new draft deal to end the war with the US and Israel, an official briefed on the negotiations said. A senior Iranian official, however, said Doha had only put forward unacceptable ideas that did not amount to an offer.

    Iran's foreign minister said on Wednesday that Tehran continued to give diplomacy a chance to succeed and raised the possibility of the seven-day plan proposed by Tehran being completed in less than a week if implemented.

    Qatar, along with Pakistan, has led mediation to end the war, which has disrupted global shipping and oil supplies and has spread to Saudi Arabia and Yemen.

    US OIL STOCKPILES

    Another factor supporting crude prices was a US federal report showing a surprise decline in stockpiles.

    The US Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday that energy firms pulled 1.9 million barrels of crude from storage during the week ended October 23.

    That was a surprise draw compared with the 15,000-barrel build analysts forecast in a Reuters poll, and was in line with the 2-million-barrel decline seen in data from the American Petroleum Institute on Tuesday, according to sources. EIA/SAPI/SENERGYUSA, ENERGYAPI

    China, meanwhile, issued a fourth batch of fuel export quotas for gasoline, diesel and jet fuel, a rare additional issue that brings the 2026 total in line with 2025 despite curbs earlier in the year, three people familiar with the matter said on Wednesday.

    Looking ahead, the market is waiting for an industry meeting on Sunday, where OPEC+ oil producers are expected to keep their December oil output targets steady, people familiar with the matter said.

    Production in most of the seven core OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman — is below their output targets. OPEC+ includes the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and allies like Russia.

    Crude oil exports from Russia's Baltic and Black Sea ports could fall next month as domestic refiners increase diesel output following a deal with Washington this month, traders said.
