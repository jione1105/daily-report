🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 29 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures ended higher on Thursday, rebounding from a four-month low set a day earlier, as reports of Chinese interest in US wheat and stronger-than-expected weekly sales lifted prices, traders said.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 settled up 4-3/4 cents at $6.03-3/4 per bushel.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 ended up 2-1/4 cents at $6.45-1/4 a bushel and Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 rose 4-3/4 cents to finish at $6.24-1/2 a bushel.

    • Wheat futures rallied from early declines on reports that Chinese buyers were seeking offers on US soft red winter wheat. Analysts speculated that such purchases could help Beijing meet a $17 billion purchase commitment that the White House said China agreed to in May.

    • Strong weekly US export data was supportive as well. The US Department of Agriculture reported net export sales of US 2026-27 wheat in the week to October 22 at 849,800 metric tons, a marketing-year high that topped a range of trade expectations for 200,000 to 500,000 tons.

    • Managed funds hold a sizable net short position in CBOT wheat futures, leaving the market primed for a short-covering bounce.

    • Market players continued to monitor activity in the Black Sea grain export region after Russia allowed grain export vessels to leave the Sea of Azov for the first time since July 10, improving global supply prospects.

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 29 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures ended narrowly mixed on Thursday as pressure from the ongoing US corn harvest offset spillover strength from wheat futures, traders said.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 ended down 1/2 cent at $4.51-1/4 per bushel, paring losses after hitting $4.47-1/2, its lowest level since July 10.

    • March corn CH27 ended down 1/4 cent at $4.64-3/4 while May corn CK27 ended up 1/4 cent at $4.73.

    • Corn futures traded higher at times, following an upturn in wheat tied to reports that China was seeking offers on US wheat.

    • Analysts speculated that such purchases, coupled with potential demand for corn, could help Beijing meet a $17 billion purchase commitment that the White House said China agreed to in May.

    • However, no Chinese grain purchases were confirmed.

    • Meanwhile, harvest-related pressure hung over the market as Midwest farmers continued to bring in crops ahead of wet weather expected next week.

    • The US Department of Agriculture reported net export sales of US corn in the week ended October 22 at 1,303,900 metric tons, in line with trade expectations for 800,000 to 1,600,000 tons.

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 29 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures closed lower on Thursday on pressure from the harvest of a projected record-large US crop, along with declines in soymeal and early weakness in crude oil futures, traders said.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 settled down 4-3/4 cents, or 0.38%, at $12.19-3/4 per bushel and most-active January soybeans SF27 ended down 3-1/4 cents, or 0.26%, at $12.34-3/4, after hitting $12.26-1/2, its lowest since August 17.

    • Some traders were liquidating November soybean futures positions before Friday, which is the first notice day for deliveries against the contract.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 ended down $6.40, or 1.91%, at $329.30 per ton, after falling to $326.60, its lowest since August 24.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 rose 0.08 cent, or 0.12%, to finish at 65.64 cents per pound.

    • The US Department of Agriculture reported net export sales of US soybeans in the week ended October 22 at 2,576,400 metric tons, in line with trade expectations for 1,800,000 to 3,000,000 tons.

    • The US soybean harvest was winding down but Midwest farmers continued to bring in crops ahead of wet weather expected next week.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update for market close

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 29 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures slid more than 1% on Thursday to a nearly three-week low, extending losses for a fourth straight session, as weaker rival edible oils and worries over rising October inventories weighed.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange slid 69 ringgit, or 1.47%, to 4,616 ringgit ($1,131.32) a metric ton, the lowest closing price since October 9.

    The market traded sharply lower following the drastic drop in rival oilseeds amid the absence of any announcement on the removal of Malaysia's crude palm oil export tax, a Kuala Lumpur-based trader said.

    "Market participants remain cautious also amid concerns over potentially higher palm oil end-stocks in October, as indicated by rising production in the near term," the trader added.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 slipped 0.71%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 shed 2.38%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were down 1.46%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    By Georgina McCartney

    HOUSTON, Oct 29 (Reuters) - Oil prices fell on Thursday in a choppy session as hopes for peace talks between the US and Iran offset some concerns over Middle East supply disruptions from an escalating regional conflict.

    Brent crude futures LCOc1 settled down 44 cents, or 0.38%, to $114.88 a barrel. US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude CLc1 futures settled down 78 cents, or 0.76%, at $102.

    Both benchmarks fell by more than $2 a barrel earlier in the session, unwinding some of Brent's 1.3% and WTI's 3% Wednesday gains.

    US oil firms ExxonMobil XOM.N and Chevron CVX.N have cancelled plans for their executives to travel to the United Arab Emirates' biggest oil show next week, citing security concerns, two sources familiar with the developments told Reuters.

    Yemen's Houthis on Thursday said they attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, Rabigh oil refinery, an Aramco facility in Abqaiq and airports in the country. There was no immediate confirmation from Saudi Arabia on the Houthi statement.

    Those reports of a new spate of attacks helped oil recoup some losses later in the session, said John Kilduff, partner at Again Capital.

    Earlier, US Vice President JD Vance sought to revive peace negotiations by sending a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, the Wall Street Journal said on Wednesday, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter.

    "Oil prices are under pressure today amid renewed diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the conflict in the Middle East," UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo said.

    Qatari mediators have presented Iran with a new draft deal to end the war, an official briefed on the negotiations said, but a senior Iranian official dismissed it as unacceptable ideas that did not amount to an offer.

    Iran's foreign minister said on Wednesday Tehran continued to give diplomacy a chance to succeed and flagged the possibility of completing its seven-day plan in less than a week if adopted.

    Concerns over intensified attacks by the Iran-aligned Houthis and fears the Trump administration may launch an attack on Iran following the US midterm elections on November 3 limited the decline.

    The US military's senior officer overseeing the Middle East met US President Donald Trump at the White House on Thursday to outline military options in Iran, the Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, citing US officials and other people with direct knowledge of the meeting.

    The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said it had destroyed a ballistic missile fired by the group towards the kingdom's eastern region early on Thursday, as hostilities between the two sides show no signs of abating.

    "The Saudi-led coalition's interception of another Houthi missile early Thursday shows that threats to regional infrastructure remain active despite the renewed push for negotiations," Gelber and Associates analysts said in a note on Thursday.

    The Houthis attacked Aden's airport on Wednesday, Yemeni government forces said.

    On Wednesday, the Houthis said they hit Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery with a missile, stepping up strikes against the world's second-largest crude producer after the US.

    Meanwhile, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said that three tankers hit sea mines, exploded and caught fire while attempting to use an unauthorised route south of the Strait of Hormuz over the past 24 hours.

    Some expectations of rising crude and diesel production from Russia also kept a lid on prices.

    "There are mixed signals coming from Russia about what they are going to do about fulfilling their diesel promise to the US, that is helping keep prices aloft," Kilduff said.

    There have been no further confirmed attacks on Russian refining infrastructure since the October 15 strike on the Ryazan refinery, JPMorgan analysts said in a note late on Wednesday. "The silence has allowed Russian refinery runs to rebound by 350,000 barrels a day last week, to 4 million barrels a day."

  • Oct 29 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, rose to its highest in more than two weeks on Thursday as capesize rates jumped.
