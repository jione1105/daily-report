🌾 Wheat Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 30 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade wheat futures edged lower on Friday and set a ninth consecutive weekly loss, as prices remained pressured by prospects of increased Russian wheat exports, and despite strong demand for US grain and reports that China could buy from the United States, market analysts said.

    • CBOT December soft red winter wheat WZ26 settled down 1/4 cent at $6.03-1/2 per bushel.

    • The most-active contract on a continuous basis Wv1 ended down more than 3% for the week, and has fallen more than 23% over the past nine weeks.

    • K.C. December hard red winter wheat KWZ26 ended down 1 cent at $6.44-1/4 a bushel and Minneapolis December spring wheat MWEZ26 fell 3/4 cent at $6.23-3/4 a bushel.

    • Shipments from the Black Sea could increase after sources said Russia, the world's largest wheat exporter, had allowed grain export vessels to leave the Sea of Azov for the first time since July 10.

    • However, attacks continue in the region, with a Greek container ship sunk on Thursday. On the demand side, Bloomberg reported on Thursday that buyers in China, the world's biggest crop importer, were seeking offers on US soft red winter wheat.

    • Traders have also been keeping a close eye on both geopolitical stress and investor positioning ahead of next week's US election, said market analysts.

    • And managed funds hold a sizable net short position in CBOT wheat futures, leaving the market primed for a short-covering bounce.


    (Reporting by P.J. Huffstutter)

🌽 Corn Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 30 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade corn futures fell on Friday, with the most-active contract Cv1 marking a fifth weekly loss, on growing US harvest supplies and fund liquidation as traders adjusted positions ahead of month end, analysts said.

    • CBOT December corn CZ26 ended down 3-1/2 cents at $4.47-3/4 per bushel.

    • March corn CH27 ended down 3-3/4 cents at $4.61 a bushel and May corn CK27 ended 4 cents lower at $4.69 a bushel.

    • The most-active contract on a continuous basis Cv1 ended down 1.81% for the week, and has fallen 15.2% over the past five weeks.

    • Harvest-related pressure hung over the market as Midwest farmers continued to bring in crops ahead of wet weather expected next week.

    • Still, rain showers over the weekend and early next week may stall harvest progress, one analyst note said.

    • Basis bids for corn were steady to lower in the US Midwest on Friday as the harvest chugged along.

    • The US soybean harvest was 79% complete and corn was 60% complete as of Sunday, the US Department of Agriculture reported, and merchandisers said progress was farther along in many areas.


    (Reporting by P.J. Huffstutter; Editing by David Gregorio)

🌱 Soybean Commentary

  • CHICAGO, Oct 30 (Reuters) - Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures ended mixed on Friday on bargain-buying and signs of export demand, with deferred months ending lower, traders said.

    • While the most-active contract Sv1 was up for much of the session, it eased late in the day and marked a third straight weekly loss.

    • CBOT November soybeans SX26 settled up 2-1/2 cents at $12.22-1/4 per bushel and most-active January soybeans SF27 ended down 1/2 cent at $12.34-1/4.

    • Some traders this week have been liquidating November soybean futures positions ahead of Friday, which is the first notice day for deliveries against the contract.

    • CBOT December soymeal SMZ26 ended down $4.70 at $324.60 per ton, after falling to $324.40, its lowest since August 21.

    • CBOT December soyoil BOZ26 rose 0.26 cent to finish at 65.90 cents per pound.

    • Exporters sold 165,150 metric tons of US soybeans received in the reporting period to unknown destinations for 2026/27 delivery, the US Department of Agriculture said in its daily reporting system on Friday.

    • USDA also reported that an additional 132,000 tons of US soybeans were sold to unknown destinations for the 2026/2027 marketing year.

    • The US soybean harvest was winding down but Midwest farmers continued to bring in crops ahead of wet weather expected next week, analysts said.

🛢 Vegetable Oil Commentary

  • Update with closing prices

    KUALA LUMPUR, Oct 30 (Reuters) - Malaysian palm oil futures closed more than 1% higher on Friday, booking a fourth monthly rise in five, as bargain buying and firmer Dalian palm olein and Chicago soyoil supported prices.

    The benchmark palm oil contract FCPOc3 for January delivery on the Bursa Malaysia Derivatives Exchange gained 66 ringgit, or 1.43%, to 4,682 ringgit ($1,155.19) a metric ton at the close.

    The contract rose 1.56% this month after tumbling 5.8% in September, lifted by firmer crude oil prices and strong export demand, even as concerns over higher stockpiles weighed on prices.

    Crude palm oil futures traded higher on the day following the recovery in Dalian palm olein and Chicago soyoil during Asian hours, said Anilkumar Bagani, head of research at Mumbai-based vegetable oil broker Sunvin Group.

    "The prices also recovered on bargain buying with strong technical support seen below 4,600 ringgit for the benchmark January 2027 futures," Bagani said.

    Bagani added that the market's focus has now turned to Malaysia's October full-month palm oil export and production data scheduled for November 10, as well as the expected rise in stocks.

    Dalian's most-active soyoil contract DBYcv1 rose 0.54%, while its palm oil contract DCPcv1 added 0.9%. Soyoil prices on the Chicago Board of Trade BOcv1 were up 0.76%.

    Palm oil tracks the price movements of rival edible oils, as it competes for a share of the global vegetable oils market.

    Oil prices rose and were on track for a fourth monthly gain as traders braced for a potential escalation in the US-Iran conflict after US midterm elections, with an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz highlighting supply risks from the war. O/R

    Stronger crude oil futures make palm a more attractive option for biodiesel feedstock.

    The ringgit MYR=, palm's currency of trade, strengthened 0.07% against the dollar, making the commodity more expensive for buyers holding foreign currencies.

    ($1 = 4.0530 ringgit)

🛢 Crude Oil Commentary

  • Updates with settlement price

    By Arathy Somasekhar

    HOUSTON, Oct 30 (Reuters) - Oil prices jumped more than 2% on Friday and settled at their highest levels since the end of April, as traders braced for a potential escalation in the Iran war after the US midterm elections, with an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz highlighting supply risks.

    Brent crude futures for December LCOc1, which expired on Friday, settled up $2.76, or 2.4%, at $117.64 a barrel, after touching a high of $118. The more active January contract LCOc2 gained $2.63 to $113.43.

    US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude futures CLc1 settled $2.12, or 2.08%, higher at $104.12.

    For the month, Brent finished 13.6% higher, while WTI rose 15.2%.

    "Traders will remain focused on oil flows out of the Middle East and the likely escalation by US and allied forces," said Dennis Kissler, senior vice president of trading at BOK Financial.

    On Friday, a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was hit by explosive devices attached by a small boat, said the UKMTO, the British maritime security service. It was not immediately clear who was behind the attack in the strait, the key sea outlet for Gulf crude and fuel supplies.

    It was the first known successful strike in the waterway using limpet mines, explosives that are attached to a ship's hull from a small boat, since the US-Israeli war with Iran began in February, maritime sources said.

    'HIGHER LIKELIHOOD OF AN ESCALATION'

    The US ordered non-emergency government employees and their family members to leave Saudi Arabia effective October 23 due to safety risks, the US embassy in Riyadh said on Friday, citing Houthi attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure.

    Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi militants said on Thursday they had attacked several sites in Saudi Arabia, including the large Ras Tanura oil refinery, the Rabigh refinery and a production facility in Abqaiq owned by state oil company Saudi Aramco.

    A Saudi-led coalition also said it had intercepted a cruise missile launched by the Houthis towards the kingdom's western region.

    Meanwhile, Qatari mediators returned to Tehran on Friday as part ​of coordinated efforts to reach a deal ‌between the US and Iran, an official briefed on the talks told Reuters.

    US President Donald Trump said on Thursday that Vice President JD Vance had reached out to Iran to help the country get medical supplies, after US media reported Vance had written to Tehran's top security official to revive peace talks.

    Iran has received a letter from Vance, but it has not responded to it yet, Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency reported, adding that Tehran wants the US to meet its conditions and "any position that contradicts this framework is unacceptable."

    Oil prices could become even more volatile in the next couple of weeks due to Israel's recent elections and the US midterm elections on November 3, according to a Saxo Bank briefing on Thursday.

    The Danish bank said crude is expected to trade in a higher range of $70 to $90 a barrel even after the conflict ends, compared with a pre-war range of $60 to $70.

    SAUDI EXPORTS RISE

    Saudi Arabia's crude exports rose in October from September, two of three ship-tracking companies said, despite Iranian attacks on shipping in the Gulf and claims by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis of attacks on Saudi oil facilities.

    The US oil rig count, an indicator of future output, rose by two. US crude oil output increased in August, while demand for crude oil and petroleum products declined, the US Energy Information Administration (EIA) said in its Petroleum Supply Monthly report on Friday.

    Meanwhile, Libya's National Oil Corporation said an armed group shut a valve on Friday on the main Hamada crude oil pipeline, which links the Hamada field to the Zawiya refinery. It said the closure of the valve had halted the flow of about 15,000 barrels per day.

🚢 Dry Bulk Freight Commentary

  • Oct 30 (Reuters) - The Baltic Exchange's main sea freight index, which tracks rates for ships carrying dry bulk commodities, was poised for a second monthly loss on Friday, dragged lower by continued weakness in capesize and panamax vessel rates.

    • The main Baltic index .BADI, which tracks rates for capesize, panamax and supramax vessels, ticked up by 2 points, or 0.07%, to 2,714 points, its highest since October 13

    • However, the index is on track for a second straight monthly decline, down more than 12% in October

    • The capesize index .BACI rose by 36 points, or 0.88%, to 4,126 points, the index's highest since October 12. However, it is down 16%, its second monthly decline

    • Average daily earnings for capesize vessels .BATCA, which typically transport 150,000-ton cargoes, including iron ore and coal, increased by $325 to $33,917

    • Iron ore futures fell on Friday, paring some of the previous day's gains, as port inventories rebounded and steel mill profitability stayed low IRONORE/

    • The panamax index .BPNI fell 32 points, or 1.67%, to 1,889 points — its lowest level since April 13. The index is headed for a monthly decline of about 20.76%

    • Average daily earnings for panamax vessels .BPWT, which usually carry 60,000 to 70,000 tons of coal or grain, fell by $290 to $16,999

    • Among smaller vessels, the supramax index .BSIS inched down 5 points, or 0.27%, to 1,865 points, hitting its lowest since October 15, but on track for a third consecutive monthly gain, up 3.8%
